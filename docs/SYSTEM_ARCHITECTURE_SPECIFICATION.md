# Coimbatore Smart City Digital Twin
## Predictive Traffic, Energy & Urban Intelligence Platform
### System Architecture & Technical Specification Document

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Project Understanding

The **Coimbatore Smart City Digital Twin** is a software-only, cyber-physical urban intelligence platform representing the key physical, temporal, and infrastructural dynamics of Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu, India. 

The platform models urban flow across three core pillars:
1. **Intelligent Mobility & Traffic Dynamics**: Road network graph analysis, real-time congestion tracking, multi-horizon ML forecasting (15m, 30m, 60m, 120m), SHAP-driven cause attribution, downstream impact graph propagation, and "what-if" detour/road-closure simulations.
2. **Energy Grid & Power Demand**: Substation/zonal demand monitoring, diurnal-seasonal peak forecasting (1h, 3h, 6h, 12h, 24h), weather-load sensitivity analysis, and grid stress what-if scenarios.
3. **Environmental Intelligence**: Spatial air quality index (AQI) tracking, PM2.5/PM10/NO2 dispersion estimation, and correlation modeling with stop-and-go traffic emissions.

All components are contextualized for Coimbatore's actual geographic corridors:
- **Avinashi Road (NH 544)**: Hope College, Peelamedu, TIDEL Park, KMCH, Airport Junction.
- **Trichy Road (NH 81)**: Ramanathapuram, Sungam Bypass, Singanallur Bus Junction, Ondipudur.
- **Sathy Road (NH 948)**: Gandhipuram, Ganapathy, Saravanampatti IT Corridor.
- **Mettupalayam Road (NH 181)**: Saibaba Colony, Kavundampalayam, Thudiyalur.
- **Pollachi Road (NH 83)**: Ukkadam Junction, Eachanari.
- **Commercial Cores**: Cross Cut Road, 100 Feet Road, D.B. Road (RS Puram), Town Hall.

## 5. Mathematical & Algorithmic Formulations

### 5.1 Congestion Scoring Mechanism
Congestion index $C_i(t) \in [0, 100]$ is computed using the Greenshields traffic density equilibrium:

$$
C_i(t) = \min\left(100, \left( 0.55 \cdot \max\left(0, \frac{v_{\text{free}} - v(t)}{v_{\text{free}}}\right) + 0.35 \cdot \frac{k(t)}{k_{\text{jam}}} + 0.10 \cdot \frac{q(t)}{L_i} \right) \times 100 \right)
$$

Where:
- $v(t)$ is current average vehicular speed.
- $v_{\text{free}}$ is the free-flow speed limit (e.g., 50 km/h).
- $k(t)$ is vehicular density (vehicles per km per lane).
- $k_{\text{jam}}$ is jam density ($\approx 125$ vehicles/km/lane).
- $q(t)$ is physical queue buildup length in meters.
- $L_i$ is total segment length in meters.

**Categorical Thresholds**:
- **LOW**: $0 \le C_i(t) < 30$ (Fluid flow, travel time factor $< 1.15\times$)
- **MODERATE**: $30 \le C_i(t) < 60$ (Dense flow, travel time factor $1.15\times - 1.50\times$)
- **HIGH**: $60 \le C_i(t) < 85$ (Stop-and-go bottleneck, travel time factor $1.50\times - 2.50\times$)
- **SEVERE**: $85 \le C_i(t) \le 100$ (Gridlock, travel time factor $> 2.50\times$, queue spillover)

### 5.2 Downstream Network Flow Propagation Algorithm
When a bottleneck or incident occurs on segment $e_0$ with capacity drop $\Delta C_0$:
1. Upstream segments $U(e_0)$ undergo queue accumulation:
   $$\frac{dq_{u}}{dt} = \text{Demand}_{\text{in}}(t) - \min(\text{Capacity}_{e_0}(t), \text{Capacity}_u)$$
2. Drivers dynamically divert to parallel routes using Logit Choice model probabilities:
   $$P(R_k) = \frac{\exp(-\theta \cdot T_k)}{\sum_j \exp(-\theta \cdot T_j)}$$
3. Alternate corridors (e.g., Trichy Road receiving overflow from Avinashi Road) absorb volume $\Delta V_k = V_{\text{diverted}} \cdot P(R_k)$, increasing their congestion score.
4. Fuel consumption and emissions are estimated via the EPA Vehicle Specific Power (VSP) model:
   $$\Delta \text{CO}_2 = \sum \text{Distance}_k \times (\text{Idle\_Factor} \times \text{Delay}_{\text{seconds}} + \text{Emission\_Factor}(v))$$

## 6. Machine Learning Pipelines

### 6.1 Multi-Horizon Traffic Prediction
- **Input Features**:
  - Temporal: `hour_sin`, `hour_cos`, `day_of_week`, `is_weekend`, `is_holiday_tamilnadu`.
  - Spatial-Lag: `speed_lag_15m`, `speed_lag_30m`, `speed_lag_60m`, `upstream_segment_speed`.
  - Environmental: `temperature`, `precipitation_mm`, `visibility_km`.
  - Incident: `incident_active_flag`, `capacity_reduction_ratio`.
- **Target**: Predicted congestion score $C(t + H)$ for $H \in \{15, 30, 60, 120\}$ minutes.
- **Model**: LightGBM Regressor with early stopping, evaluated using MAE and RMSE.
- **Explainability**: TreeSHAP values calculated at inference time, yielding normalized percentage contribution for each factor.

### 6.2 Energy Demand Forecasting
- **Input Features**:
  - Temporal: `hour_of_day`, `day_of_week`, `month`, `is_summer_peak`.
  - Meteorological: `temp_celsius`, `cooling_degree_days` ($\max(0, T - 24)$), `humidity_index`.
  - Historical Lags: `demand_mw_lag_1h`, `demand_mw_lag_24h`, `demand_mw_lag_168h` (same hour last week).
  - Industrial Shifts: Proxies for Peelamedu/Kurichi industrial operating shifts.
- **Model**: XGBoost Regressor with hyperparameter tuning, evaluated using MAPE ($< 3.8\%$).

## 7. AI Urban Assistant (RAG & Tool Calling)

The AI assistant utilizes an autonomous agent loop:
1. **User Query Analysis**: Classifies intent (Status lookup, Causal explanation, Predictive inquiry, What-If simulation command).
2. **Schema-Grounded Tool Execution**:
   - `get_corridor_status(corridor_name: str)`
   - `get_active_incidents(severity: str)`
   - `get_energy_peak_forecast()`
   - `run_what_if_scenario(scenario_params: dict)`
3. **Synthesis & Citation**: Grounds answers in actual database records with explicit `DATA_ORIGIN` tags.
